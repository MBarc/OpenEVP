How to connect a recorder
  1. Run a cable from the recorder's headphone jack to your PC's line-in, or to a USB audio adapter. Many recorders have a 3.5 mm jack. Some, like the Panasonic RR-DR60, have a smaller 2.5 mm one, so you may need a 2.5 mm to 3.5 mm adapter. A line-in works better than a microphone input, which is much more sensitive.
  2. Choose that input above, then play a little and set the recorder's volume to about the middle. The meter should move well, but loud parts should stay out of the red.
  3. Set the recorder's playback speed to normal, and turn voice activation off.
  4. Press Record here first, then Play on the recorder, and Stop when it is done. The import opens in the player with suggested cuts wherever the recorder went quiet for the seconds set above. Check them, remove or add cuts, then split it into separate recordings or keep it as one. The whole import is always kept.

About OpenEVP

Gets the recordings off ghost-hunting voice recorders and onto a modern PC.

OpenEVP is an independent project and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or supported by Sony or Panasonic. "Sony", "ICD-ST25", "ICD-ST10" and "Digital Voice Editor" are trademarks of Sony Corporation. "Panasonic" and "RR-DR60" are trademarks of Panasonic Corporation.

To play and convert recordings made on Sony IC recorders, OpenEVP includes numeric tables needed to read Sony's LPEC audio formats (LPEC LP, used by the ICD-ST25 and ICD-ST10, and LPEC SP and LPEC ST, used by the ICD-ST10). They are included only so owners can access their own recordings (interoperability). Rights holders who object can open an issue at https://github.com/MBarc/OpenEVP/issues and the tables will be removed promptly.

MP3 clips are encoded with lameenc (LGPL-3.0-or-later), which includes the LAME MP3 encoder (LGPL-2.0-or-later). MP3 files are decoded with minimp3 (CC0, public domain). USB access uses libusb (LGPL-2.1).

The Leveler (Enhance) is a port of the Chromium web audio compressor, Copyright (C) 2011 Google Inc., used under the BSD 3-Clause licence; its full text is in the LICENSES folder installed with OpenEVP.
